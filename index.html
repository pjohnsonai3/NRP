Flagler Arts District

A place where life is intentionally cultivated — through food, landscape, architecture, and shared experience. CERES’ initial phase includes four commercial buildings, each playing a distinct but interdependent role; two retail buildings activate the public realm through color and visible interiors, their architecture influenced by classic French design with a colonnade marching along the street front. At its center, the Incubator — the engine of innovation — showcases chefs, experimentation, and culinary culture, designed as a lantern that glows at night between New Orleans–inspired columns and ample outdoor seating.

Ceres

A wellness retreat in Fort Lauderdale shaped by light, water and natural texture. Guests arrive through a warm reception screened in timber lattice, then move along softly lit passages of limestone, wood and plaster to steam, sauna and quiet relaxation lounges. At the heart of the plan, a sculptural stair spirals above a reflecting pool, drawing daylight down through the building. Fitness, retail and lounge spaces share the same calm, sun-washed palette — a place to slow down, restore and reconnect.

Considered one of the largest urban infill sites in Atlanta, the development is organized in a “town square setting,” and will pay homage to the industrial roots of the area. Set in historic downtown Fredericksburg on the site of the city’s oldest newspaper, The Free Lance–Star, The Publisher takes its story from the printed word. The interiors draw on Fredericksburg’s patriotic past with bold contrasts and locally sourced art — a sophisticated lobby, a destination bar and restaurant, and guest rooms with channeled leather headboards and campaign-detailed casegoods. A Tribute Portfolio hotel meant to be the town’s new central connector, where guests and locals come to share their own stories.

The Publisher

Rising on Mile Zero, the historic terminus of the Western & Atlantic Railroad where Atlanta began, Hotel Phoenix is the first hotel to open at Centennial Yards, the 50-acre redevelopment of downtown’s former rail yards. The 19-story, 292-room hotel draws on the city’s rail history, balancing a modern industrial edge with the civic landmarks around it. Guest rooms take their cue from luxury train travel: fluted panels, tufting and leather strap details, walnut with bronze accents and sculptural smoked-glass lighting, all framed by views of the skyline and Mercedes-Benz Stadium.

Rooted in the storied traditions of outdoor sporting life and inspired by its namesake text, St. Albans Sporting Club is an urban retreat; where the rugged great outdoors meets the refinement of elevated hospitality. At St. Albans, discerning hunters, anglers, and adventure seekers will find a gathering place that honors their pursuits — a destination where tales of the field are exchanged over exceptional cuisine, and where every detail evokes both reverence for nature and the thrill of what lies beyond the next trailhead. The interiors draw from the quiet majesty of the English countryside, subtly nodding to Tudor sensibilities — using pastoral tapestry inspired artwork, live-sawn wood beams, and textured flagstone floors. These timeworn legacy materials are elevated by sleek metal accents, contemporary furnishings, and integrated lighting — merging old-world charm with a modern explorer’s edge. A natural palette, pulled from riverbeds, forests, and upland fields, grounds the space in authenticity, while safari-inspired elements and bespoke, globally inspired details speak to a spirit of wanderlust and exploration. At once sophisticated and welcoming, St. Albans offers members a “base camp” in the heart of the city — a space that aims to “honor both nature and tradition” (The Book of St. Albans), while fostering connection and camaraderie amongst fellow stewards of the wild.

St. Albans Sporting Club

As an effervescent, community-minded wine club, The Perlant celebrates and educates members in the beauty found within the subtle nuances and terroir of each bottle. Sowed within the soil of a bustling, ever-evolving metropolis, it radiates as a symbol of serenity — an undeniable invitation inward to a communal moment of reprieve, where the club’s cultural core lives in humility, generosity, and kindness. Fluid forms inspired by the organic curves of a grapevine meet rich textures and warm tones; light woods are accented with washed rust and sage, and textured plaster finishes are bathed in soft, filtered light. Above all, The Perlant yields an inclusive environment for both the wine novice and the connoisseur to rendezvous for the sake of collective enrichment and delight.

City rule

Applications are due five Fridays before the DRC meeting. Staff case review occurs three Wednesdays before the meeting. The DRC comment report is issued the Friday before the meeting.

What this changes

We move the preliminary City touchpoint into Concept Design and target the DRC application for Feb. 5 so the 10-week Phase 2 window can include a projected Mar. 9 DRC meeting. Post-meeting resubmittal and final sign-off remain authority-controlled.
